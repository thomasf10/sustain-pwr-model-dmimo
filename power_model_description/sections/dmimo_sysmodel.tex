\section{Distributed Massive MIMO System Model}

\subsection{Downlink System Model}

We consider the downlink of a distributed (cell-free) massive \gls{mimo}
network in which $L$ \glspl{ap}, each equipped with $M$ antennas, jointly serve
$K$ single-antenna users over the same time-frequency resources. The APs are
connected through fronthaul links to a central processing unit (CPU) that
encodes the data and computes the precoders, so that the $LM$ antennas act as a
single distributed array. Transmission uses OFDM with $Q$ data subcarriers,
indexed by $q=1,\dots,Q$; the model below is stated per subcarrier and the same
processing is applied on each of them.

Let $\vect{h}_{kl}[q]\inC{M}$ denote the channel from AP $l$ to user $k$ on
subcarrier $q$. Stacking the contributions of all APs gives the collective
channel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:collective-channel}
\vect{h}_{k}[q] =
\begin{bmatrix} \vect{h}_{k1}[q] \\ \vdots \\ \vect{h}_{kL}[q] \end{bmatrix}
\inC{LM},
\end{equation}
which we model as correlated Rayleigh fading,
$\vect{h}_{k}[q]\sim\cn{\vect{0}_{LM}}{\mat{R}_{k}[q]}$. Because the antennas are
geographically distributed, the spatial correlation matrix is block-diagonal,
$\mat{R}_{k}[q]=\diag{\mat{R}_{k1}[q],\dots,\mat{R}_{kL}[q]}\inC{LM\times LM}$,
with each block $\mat{R}_{kl}[q]\inC{M\times M}$ describing the correlation
across the antennas of AP $l$; its scaling captures the large-scale fading
between AP $l$ and user $k$.

The CPU maps the data symbol $s_{k}[q]\in\mathbb{C}$ of user $k$, with unit
power $\expt{\abs{s_{k}[q]}^2}=1$ and mutually independent across users, onto the
$M$ antennas of each AP. AP $l$ transmits
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ap-transmit}
\vect{x}_{l}[q] = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \vect{w}_{kl}[q]\, s_{k}[q]
= \mat{W}_{l}[q]\,\vect{s}[q],
\end{equation}
where $\vect{w}_{kl}[q]\inC{M}$ is the precoding vector that AP $l$ applies to
user $k$, $\vect{s}[q]=[\,s_{1}[q]\ \cdots\ s_{K}[q]\,]^{\intercal}\inC{K}$
collects the data symbols, and
$\mat{W}_{l}[q]=[\,\vect{w}_{1l}[q]\ \cdots\ \vect{w}_{Kl}[q]\,]\inC{M\times K}$
is the precoding matrix of AP $l$. Collecting the per-AP precoders of a given
user into
$\vect{w}_{k}[q]=[\,\vect{w}_{k1}[q]^{\intercal}\ \cdots\ \vect{w}_{kL}[q]^{\intercal}\,]^{\intercal}\inC{LM}$
yields the network-wide precoding matrix
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network-precoder}
\mat{W}[q] = \big[\,\vect{w}_{1}[q]\ \cdots\ \vect{w}_{K}[q]\,\big]
\inC{LM\times K},
\end{equation}
whose $l$-th block of $M$ rows equals $\mat{W}_{l}[q]$, so that the stacked
transmit vector is
$\vect{x}[q]=[\,\vect{x}_{1}[q]^{\intercal}\ \cdots\ \vect{x}_{L}[q]^{\intercal}\,]^{\intercal}=\mat{W}[q]\,\vect{s}[q]\inC{LM}$.

The signal received by user $k$ on subcarrier $q$ is
$y_{k}[q]=\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{x}[q]+n_{k}[q]$, which, using
\eqref{eq:network-precoder}, separates into the desired term, the multi-user
interference, and the noise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:downlink-received}
y_{k}[q] =
\underbrace{\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{k}[q]\, s_{k}[q]}_{\text{desired signal}}
+ \underbrace{\sum_{\substack{i=1 \\ i\neq k}}^{K}
\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{i}[q]\, s_{i}[q]}_{\text{multi-user interference}}
+ \underbrace{\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{K}} n_{k}[q]}_{\text{noise}},
\end{equation}
where $n_{k}[q]\sim\cn{0}{\sigma^{2}}$ is the receiver noise with power
$\sigma^{2}$, independent across users and subcarriers and independent of the
channels and data. The scalar $\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{k}[q]$ is the
effective channel seen by user $k$: the distributed precoder turns the
$LM$-antenna channel into an equivalent single-antenna link.

Unlike co-located massive MIMO, where a single sum-power budget applies to the
whole array, each AP is a separate transmitter and is subject to its own power
constraint. Since the data symbols are independent and unit-power, the average
power radiated by AP $l$ across the OFDM block is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:power-constraint}
\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\expt{\norm{\vect{x}_{l}[q]}^{2}}
= \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\expt{\norm{\vect{w}_{kl}[q]}^{2}}
\leq \rho_{\max}, \qquad l=1,\dots,L,
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\max}$ is the maximum downlink transmit power per AP, assumed equal
for all APs, and the expectation is taken over the data and channel
realizations.

It is convenient to split each precoder into a transmit direction and a power
control coefficient,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:precoder-decomposition}
\vect{w}_{k}[q] = \sqrt{\rho_{k}}\,
\frac{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]}{\sqrt{\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]}^{2}}}},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]\inC{LM}$ is an arbitrarily scaled vector that fixes
the spatial direction of the transmission to user $k$ and $\rho_{k}\geq 0$ is the
downlink power allocated to that user, so that
$\expt{\norm{\vect{w}_{k}[q]}^{2}}=\rho_{k}$. This separation isolates the two
design tasks treated below: the choice of the precoding directions
$\{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]\}$ (\Cref{sssec:precoding-schemes}) and the choice of the
power allocation $\{\rho_{k}\}$ (\Cref{sssec:power-control}), the latter being
what ties the per-user powers to the per-AP budget in
\eqref{eq:power-constraint}.

Treating the multi-user interference in \eqref{eq:downlink-received} as noise,
the effective downlink \gls{sinr} of user $k$ on subcarrier $q$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:downlink-sinr}
\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{dl}}[q] =
\frac{\abs{\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{k}[q]}^{2}}
{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{i=1 \\ i\neq k}}^{K}
\abs{\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{i}[q]}^{2} + \sigma^{2}} .
\end{equation}
With the effective channel known at the receiver, an achievable rate for user
$k$ on subcarrier $q$ is $\log_{2}\!\big(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{dl}}[q]\big)$,
and the delivered downlink sum rate $R_{\mathrm{DL}}$ follows by inserting these
per-user, per-subcarrier SINRs into the ergodic rate expression of
\Cref{sec:rate} (\eqref{eq:rate}), which in turn drives the encoder and MIMO
processing consumption of the power model.

\subsubsection{Transmit Precoding Schemes}
\label{sssec:precoding-schemes}

The precoding directions can be computed in two fundamentally different ways,
mirroring the centralized and distributed uplink architectures of
\cite{demir2021foundations}. In \emph{centralized} operation the APs forward
their received pilots to the CPU, which estimates the collective channels and
designs all directions jointly. Because it sees the whole array, the CPU can
make the serving APs cancel one another's interference, giving up to $LM$
spatial degrees of freedom for suppressing the interference to each user. The
nearly optimal choice is minimum mean-squared-error (\gls{mmse}) precoding, the
downlink dual of \gls{mmse} combining, which balances the signal power delivered
to a user against the interference it causes; its complexity grows with $K$, so
it is not scalable. Scalable approximations restrict the computation to the
users that share serving APs with the target user, giving the P-MMSE and P-RZF
precoders, which retain most of the interference-suppression ability at a
bounded per-user cost. Removing the channel-estimate regularization altogether
yields regularized zero-forcing and, in the limit, the \gls{zf} precoder
\begin{align}
\label{eq:zf-precoder}
\bar{\mat{W}}[q] &= \mat{H}[q]\big(\mat{H}[q]^{H}\mat{H}[q]\big)^{-1},  \\
\mat{H}[q]&=[\,\vect{h}_{1}[q]\ \cdots\ \vect{h}_{K}[q]\,]\in \mathbb{C}^{LM\times K},
\end{align}
whose columns $\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]$ null the inter-user interference when the
\gls{csi} is accurate. Zero-forcing is the scheme adopted in the power model of
\Cref{sec:pwr_model}; \gls{rzf}, obtained by adding a loading term
$\mat{H}[q](\mat{H}[q]^{H}\mat{H}[q]+\lambda\mat{I}_{K})^{-1}$, trades some
interference leakage for robustness at low SNR. In practice the CPU builds
$\mat{H}[q]$ from the channel estimates acquired on the uplink pilots, exploiting
TDD reciprocity so that no downlink pilots are needed.

In \emph{distributed} operation the CPU only encodes the data, and each AP forms
its own directions $\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q]$ from its locally estimated channels
$\hat{\vect{h}}_{kl}[q]$, the MMSE estimates of $\vect{h}_{kl}[q]$. An AP can then
suppress only the interference it generates itself, with the $M$ spatial degrees
of freedom of its own array, so the coherent cross-AP cancellation of the
centralized case is lost and interference must instead be kept small at each AP
individually. The local counterpart of \gls{mmse} precoding is L-MMSE precoding,
which is locally optimal but again not scalable; LP-MMSE precoding restores
scalability by summing only over the users an AP serves; and the simplest option
is maximum-ratio (\gls{mr}) precoding,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mr-precoder}
\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q] = \vect{h}_{kl}[q],
\end{equation}
which maximizes the signal power delivered to the desired user but ignores the
interference it causes, with the benefit of admitting a closed-form SINR.
Whichever architecture is used, the uplink-downlink duality guarantees that any
set of uplink combining vectors achieves the same SINRs when reused, after
normalization, as downlink precoding directions \cite{demir2021foundations}.

\subsubsection{Downlink Power Control}
\label{sssec:power-control}

The directions fix the shape of each user's beam but not how much power it
receives; that is set by the power control coefficients $\{\rho_{k}\}$, which
must satisfy the per-AP constraints in \eqref{eq:power-constraint} while
distributing a limited budget across users. Let
$\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}'[q]\inC{M}$ be the AP-$l$ block of the normalized direction
$\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]/\sqrt{\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]}^{2}}}$, so that
$\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}'[q]}^{2}}$ is the fraction of user $k$'s power
that AP $l$ radiates. The constraint \eqref{eq:power-constraint} then reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:power-constraint-decomposed}
\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\,
\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}'[q]}^{2}} \leq \rho_{\max},
\qquad l=1,\dots,L,
\end{equation}
which couples the powers of all users served by a common AP. Unlike the
directions, which are recomputed every coherence block, the power allocation
depends mainly on the large-scale fading and can be held fixed over many
coherence blocks.

The simplest policy gives every user the same power and rescales all precoders
by a single factor set by the most heavily loaded AP,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:power-normalization}
\gamma = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\max}}{\max_{l}\tilde{p}_{l}}},
\qquad
\tilde{p}_{l} = \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q]}^{2}},
\end{equation}
so that $\vect{w}_{kl}[q]=\gamma\,\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q]$. Because the same scalar
multiplies every AP, it preserves the relative amplitudes and phases across APs
and hence the interference nulling of \eqref{eq:zf-precoder}; the most heavily
loaded AP then transmits at exactly $\rho_{\max}$ while the others stay below
their budget. A per-AP scalar would load every AP fully but, by scaling each AP
differently, would perturb the coherent combining and reintroduce residual
multi-user interference, so the common factor is used whenever the
interference-free SINR in \eqref{eq:downlink-sinr} is assumed.

Equal power is wasteful when users have very different channel gains. A more
balanced choice is fractional power control, which sets each user's power from
its large-scale fading. Writing
$\beta_{kl}=\tfrac{1}{M}\mathrm{Tr}(\mat{R}_{kl}[q])$ for the average channel
gain between AP $l$ and user $k$, a common rule in distributed operation, where
AP $l$ assigns a separate power $\rho_{kl}$ to each user through
$\vect{w}_{kl}[q]=\sqrt{\rho_{kl}}\,\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q]/\sqrt{\expt{\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{kl}[q]}^{2}}}$,
is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fractional-power}
\rho_{kl} = \rho_{\max}\,
\frac{\beta_{kl}^{\,\kappa}}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\beta_{il}^{\,\kappa}},
\qquad l=1,\dots,L,
\end{equation}
so that AP $l$ splits its full power among its users according to their gains.
The exponent $\kappa$ tunes the trade-off between throughput ($\kappa>0$,
favoring strong users) and fairness ($\kappa<0$, protecting weak users), with
$\kappa=1/2$ recovering the heuristic of \cite{demir2021foundations}; the
centralized case admits the analogous per-user rule $\rho_{k}$ normalized against
the binding constraint \eqref{eq:power-constraint-decomposed}. Beyond such
heuristics, the powers can be optimized for a network utility such as max-min
fairness or sum rate, but these problems do not scale to very large networks, so
the statistics-based rules above are the practical default.
