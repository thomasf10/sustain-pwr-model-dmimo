\subsection{Parameters}
\label{ssec:params}

The evaluation parameters are split up into three groups. The deployment, channel and
frame of \Cref{sec:sysmodel} are collected in \Cref{tab:params_sys}. The
hardware constants of the \gls{ap} and of the co-located baseline are inherited from the \gls{fr3} \gls{bs} model of \cite{peschiera2026fr3} and are

Note that the number of data subcarriers is set to $Q=3000$ for the power model. However, the rate model instead evaluates the subcarrier average of \eqref{eq:se} on $64$
subcarriers and scales the resulting spectral efficiency by $\tilde B$, as evaluating all $3000$ subcarriers would consume unnecessary compute time.

\subsection{Evaluation Scenarios}
\label{ssec:scenarios}

We consider four evaluations that use the parameters of \Cref{tab:params_sys,tab:params_hw,%
tab:params_dmimo}. All of them hold the total antenna count at $LM=128$ and the total transmit budget at $P_{\mathrm{TX}}$, the two deployments cover the same
area with the same radiating hardware and the same radiated power, and differ
only in how finely that hardware is spread. Distributing therefore never adds
antennas, it only redistributes them. The amplifier rating
$P_{\max}=\rho_{\max}/M=P_{\mathrm{TX}}/(LM)$ is the same at every point of
every evaluation, so the amplifiers are physically identical hardware throughout and
any change in the amplifier consumption comes from utilisation alone. \todo{this probably captures enough so that remark 2 could be shortened or removed }
The first evaluation considers the delivered rate against the consumed power, by sweeping $P_{\mathrm{TX}}$, with the amplifiers re-sized at each point so that every point on the curve is a deployment dimensioned for the budget it is given. It is reported for the co-located baseline and for the three splits of \Cref{tab:split}, at $L\in\{32,64,128\}$, that is at $M\in\{4,2,1\}$ antennas per \gls{ap}. 
The second evaluation varies $L$ over $2,4,\dots,128$ at fixed $P_{\mathrm{TX}}$ and
reports $P_{\mathrm{net}}$, again for the three splits. The co-located baseline of
\Cref{ssec:ap} is drawn alongside it at $L=1$. 
The third evaluation considers the same sweep as evaluation two but looks at the contributions of \eqref{eq:pnet} and \eqref{eq:pap}, namely the amplifier, digital, analog, fronthaul, central-unit and synchronization blocks. 
The fourth evaluation reports the energy efficiency \eqref{eq:ee_net} over the same sweep.
Because the delivered rate is not constant along it, $P_{\mathrm{net}}$ alone
cannot say whether distributing is beneficial. The fronthaul ceiling $1/(\Pi_{\mathrm{FH}}L)$ of \Cref{rem:fh_ceiling} is drawn alongside the data-sharing splits.

\subsection{Evaluation 1: Delivered Rate against Consumed Power}
\label{ssec:eval_rate_power}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\input{figs/eval1_rate_vs_power.tex}
\caption{Delivered sum rate against network power consumption, at $LM=128$
antennas and $K=20$ \glspl{ue}. Each curve is obtained by sweeping the
total transmit budget over $P_{\mathrm{TX}}\in\{1.6,3.2,6.4,12.8,25.6,51.2\}\,\mathrm{W}$, increasing upward, with the amplifiers re-sized at every point.}
\label{fig:eval1}
\end{figure*}

\Cref{fig:eval1} illustrates the delivered rate against the consumed power, when sweeping the transmit power. Distributing the array enables a higher rate but results in a higher power consumption. At the nominal budget $P_{\mathrm{TX}}=\SI{12.8}{\watt}$ and $L=32$, centralized
\gls{dmimo} delivers \SI{69.7}{\giga\bit\per\second} against
\SI{44.4}{\giga\bit\per\second} for the co-located site, a factor $1.57$, but
draws \SI{470}{\watt} against \SI{188}{\watt}. The rate gain is overwhelmingly
in the uplink, \SI{14.9}{\giga\bit\per\second} against
\SI{4.9}{\giga\bit\per\second}, which is macro diversity working as expected:
every \gls{ue} is close to some \gls{ap}, whereas a single central array serves
the edge of the coverage area over the full path loss. 

The shape difference in the curves of the different deployments is significant. The co-located curve
is the only one that moves substantially along the power axis, from
\SI{121}{\watt} to \SI{417}{\watt} across a $32$-fold increase in the power budget. The distributed curves S1 and S2 are close to vertical: the rate climbs while $P_{\mathrm{net}}$ marginally increases. This highlights the fact that the distributed network carries a large consumed power overhead due to the fronthaul and distributed processing. Additionally, split S3 has a relatively flat rate when the transmit budget increases. Since local processing is interference-limited rather than noise-limited, raising every \gls{ap}'s power raises the interference with the signal and thus results in a stable \gls{sinr}. 

The three distributed curves have a different power consumption, mainly due to the fronthaul requirements. A link
operated under S1 carries $M$ quantized sample streams over the fronthaul in either direction by
\eqref{eq:fh_s1}, so the network transports $LM$ streams. A link operated under S2 or S3 carries the
traffic expressed in the user domain instead: $K$ uplink partial sums per
\gls{ap} according to \eqref{eq:fh_ul}, together with the downlink
payload duplicated to every \gls{ap}. Moving the precoder and the combiner out
to the \glspl{ap} therefore converts antenna-domain streams into user-domain
streams, and that conversion compresses the link only when an \gls{ap} has more
antennas than served \glspl{ue}. Considernig the scenario of $K=20$ \glspl{ue} and $M=\{4, 2, 1\}$ the fronthaul consumption of S2 and S3 are much higher than the one of S1. Split S2 is accordingly the most expensive of the three and worsens fastest with $L$, from \SI{1124}{\watt} to \SI{3962}{\watt} between $L=32$ and $L=128$ at the
nominal budget, while delivering exactly the rate of S1. S3 has a slighlty lower consumption as compared to S2, since local processing delivers a lower rate, and as such requires a lower fronthaul rate.

\Cref{fig:eval3} breaks down the total network power of \Cref{fig:eval2} into the respective blocks.

The \gls{pa} consumption under local processing (S3) is \SI{76.3}{\watt} at every $L$, exactly the co-located value: each \gls{ap} transmits $P_{T,l}/\rho_{\max}=1$ and the sizing convention of

from \SI{64.9}{\watt} at $L=2$ to \SI{28.5}{\watt} at $L=128$. The measured mean

constraint of \eqref{eq:zf-precoder} binds at the most loaded \gls{ap} and

finer the array is cut the wider that spread becomes. Notably, under centralized processing, distributing the

The digital block confirms \Cref{rem:fpga}, and it separates relocating the
processing from duplicating it. At $L=2$ the S1 network spends
\SI{48.6}{\watt} at the \glspl{ap} and \SI{22.3}{\watt} at the central unit, so
\SI{70.9}{\watt} in total against \SI{70.6}{\watt} for the co-located site:
moving the precoder and combiner off the \glspl{ap} costs essentially nothing.
The growth that follows is the loss of sharing. By $L=128$ the same two terms
are \SI{274.8}{} and \SI{22.4}{\watt}, a factor $4.2$ over the baseline, while
the central unit itself has not moved at all, since it runs $\lceil
LM/32\rceil=4$ \glspl{fpga} whatever $L$ is. S2 and S3 make every \gls{ap} run
its own precoder or combiner rather than only its baseband filter, and they pay
for it: \SI{489}{\watt} of \gls{ap} digital at $L=128$, a factor $7.0$ including
the central unit. The block grows more slowly than the \gls{fpga} count does,
$2.4$-fold at $L=32$ against the eight-fold ratio \Cref{rem:fpga} quotes,
because the per-chain arithmetic is untouched by the splitting and only the
fixed part is duplicated.

The fronthaul is the block that decides the figure, and it grows for two
different reasons that the totals alone do not separate. Under S1 it is nearly
flat, \SI{232}{\watt} at $L=2$ against \SI{362}{\watt} at $L=128$, and the
traffic term does not move at all: it is \SI{230}{\watt} at both ends, since
\eqref{eq:fh_s1} scales with $M$ and the network therefore carries $LM$ sample
streams whatever $L$ is. The whole \SI{130}{\watt} of growth is the
traffic-independent $P_{\mathrm{FH},0}/\eta_{\mathrm{FH,sc}}$ paid once per
link, which the $126$ additional links account for exactly. Under S2 and S3 that
same static term is a rounding error, \SI{132}{\watt} out of the \SI{3373}{} and
\SI{1807}{\watt} they reach at $L=128$, where the fronthaul is
\SI{85}{\percent} and \SI{74}{\percent} of everything the network consumes, and
the growth is traffic.

Two traffic terms carry it and both scale as $L$. The first is the duplicated
downlink payload $L\,R_{\mathrm{DL}}$ of \Cref{rem:fh_ceiling}, worth
\SI{2150}{\watt} for S2 and \SI{605}{\watt} for S3 at $L=128$. The second is the
uplink partial sums of \eqref{eq:fh_ul}: an \gls{ap} forwards $K$ scalars
whatever its $M$, so the network carries $LK$ of them where S1 carries $LM$
samples, and at $K=20$ the term doubles with every halving of the array, from
\SI{16.7}{\watt} at $L=2$ to \SI{1070}{\watt} at $L=128$. It is identical for
the two splits by construction. Which of the two dominates is not a property of
the sweep but of the split: the payload is \SI{64}{\percent} of the S2 fronthaul
at $L=128$, whereas for S3 the partial sums overtake it near $L=32$ and reach
\SI{59}{\percent}. Reading the growth of both splits as payload duplication
alone would therefore misdescribe S3, where past $L=32$ it is the uplink that
costs the most.

What S2 is built to save does not appear at all. The precoding coefficients of
\eqref{eq:fh_s2} scale with $M$, so their network total is pinned at $LM$
exactly as the S1 samples are, and they cost \SI{17.3}{\watt} at every point of
the sweep, under \SI{1}{\percent} of the S2 fronthaul at $L=128$. S2 pays the
payload duplication in full and recovers nothing measurable for it, which is the
conclusion \Cref{ssec:eval_rate_power} reached from the levels, now visible in
the growth. The gap between S2 and S3 is not a difference in the efficiency of
the two splits either: their partial-sum terms are equal, and the S2 fronthaul
is the larger one only because the centralized precoder delivers a payload that
keeps growing with $L$ while the local precoder delivers one that shrinks.

The remaining two blocks are small enough to be dismissed, which is itself worth
recording. Synchronization is exactly linear in $L$ at \SI{125}{\milli\watt} per
\gls{ap} and reaches \SI{16}{\watt} at $L=128$, near \SI{2}{\percent} of the S1
total there. The analog block grows by \SI{75}{\milli\watt} per additional
\gls{ap}, its own local oscillator, so it moves only from \SI{40.6}{} to
\SI{50.1}{\watt} across a sweep that multiplies the node count by $64$. Neither
the reference distribution nor the loss of the shared oscillator is a reason not
to distribute.

Taken together the panels give one answer. The cost of distributing an array is
transport and per-node fixed overhead, not radiated power: at $L=128$ under S1
the fronthaul and the digital blocks add \SI{570}{\watt} to a \SI{188}{\watt}
baseline while the amplifier bill falls by \SI{48}{\watt}. Every lever that
matters is therefore a fronthaul or a static-power lever, and the transmit
budget, which \Cref{fig:eval1} already showed to be close to a free parameter,
is not one of them. Both dominant fronthaul terms are proportional to $L$
through the number of \glspl{ap} that serve a given \gls{ue}, so restricting the
serving cluster acts on the payload duplication and on the partial sums at once.
That is the lever \Cref{rem:fh_ceiling} identifies, and it is the one this
evaluation deliberately withholds by having all $L$ \glspl{ap} serve all $K$
\glspl{ue} at every point of the sweep.

\Cref{fig:eval4} reports the fourth evaluation and answers the question the
previous two leave open. At these parameters distributing does not pay: the
co-located baseline reaches \SI{237.0}{\mega\bit\per\joule} and no distributed
configuration of any split at any $L$ comes within a fifth of it. The best
distributed point of the whole sweep is S2 at $L=4$, at
\SI{186.4}{\mega\bit\per\joule}, which is \SI{79}{\percent} of the baseline; S3
at $L=4$ reaches \SI{175.8}{} and S1 at $L=16$ reaches
\SI{156.0}{\mega\bit\per\joule}. The rate gain of \Cref{fig:eval1} is real, and
it is bought at a price that exceeds it in every configuration tested.

Every split has an interior optimum, and they do not sit at the same place. S2
and S3 peak at $L=4$, S1 at $L=16$. The difference is not propagation, since all
three see the same channel and two of them deliver the same rate; it is the
shape of the power curve. S1's consumption is flat to $L=16$ while its rate is
still climbing, so its optimum sits where the rate saturates. S2 and S3 start
paying for $L$ immediately, so theirs sits where the rate is still rising
steeply. Where to stop chopping the array is therefore a fronthaul question
before it is a spatial one.

The three curves cross, and past the crossing the efficiency ranking is exactly
the inverse of the fronthaul ranking. S1 is the least efficient split for
$L\leq 8$ and the most efficient from $L=16$ on, reaching
\SI{92.7}{\mega\bit\per\joule} at $L=128$ against \SI{17.6}{} for S2 and
\SI{8.7}{} for S3. For S1 and S2 the crossing is at the same $L$ as in
\Cref{fig:eval2} and for the same reason, since the two deliver identical rates
and their efficiencies are therefore in the exact inverse ratio of their powers.

The ceiling of \Cref{rem:fh_ceiling} holds throughout and becomes informative
only where it matters. S2 and S3 stay below $1/(\Pi_{\mathrm{FH}}L)$ at every
point, but the bound is loose where the fronthaul is not yet dominant and
tightens as it becomes so: S2 sits at \SI{9}{\percent} of the ceiling at $L=2$
and at \SI{56}{\percent} at $L=128$. S3 instead saturates near
\SI{29}{\percent} and stops approaching it, which the breakdown of
\Cref{fig:eval3} explains: the ceiling counts only the duplicated payload,
whereas the uplink partial sums of \eqref{eq:fh_ul} grow with $L$ as well and
carry more than half of the S3 fronthaul past $L=32$. A split that pays a second
$L$-proportional term cannot approach a bound derived from the first. S1 crosses
the ceiling between $L=16$ and $L=32$ and ends at three times it, which the
remark does not forbid, since S1 forwards samples rather than payload.

The margin between the best distributed point and the baseline is the fronthaul
traffic term and nothing else, which makes the sign of the comparison depend on
a quantity \Cref{tab:params_dmimo} does not source. Removing that term entirely,
$\Pi_{\mathrm{FH}}=0$, puts S1 at \SI{339}{\mega\bit\per\joule} at $L=8$ and
$L=16$, a factor $1.43$ \emph{above} the co-located baseline, and leaves S2 and
S3 above it as well over most of the sweep. The conclusion that distributing
costs efficiency is therefore a statement about
$\Pi_{\mathrm{FH}}=\SI{0.25}{\watt}$ per Gbit/s and about a network in which
every \gls{ap} serves every \gls{ue}, not a property of \gls{dmimo} as such.
