\section{D-MIMO System Model}
\label{sec:sysmodel}

We consider a \gls{tdd} \gls{dmimo} network in which $L$ \glspl{ap}, each with
$M$ antennas, jointly serve $K$ single-antenna \glspl{ue} over $Q$
\gls{ofdm} subcarriers, resulting in $LM$ total antennas. All $L$ \glspl{ap} are connected to
a \gls{cpu} by fronthaul links. The indices $l=1,\dots,L$, $m=1,\dots,M$,
$k=1,\dots,K$ and $q=1,\dots,Q$ denote the number of \glspl{ap}, antennas per \gls{ap}, \glspl{ue} and subcarriers respectively. Co-located \gls{mmimo} is the special case
$L=1$ with $M_{\mathrm{ant}}=LM$ antennas on a single site at the centre of the
coverage area. Co-located and distributed \gls{mimo} consider the same number of total antennas and radiate the same total power, they only differ in where the antennas sit.


We consider the same frame structure as in~\cite{peschiera2026fr3}. Namely, the total frame length is $T_f$ which is split into a \gls{dl} and \gls{ul} phase of length $\tau_{\mathrm{DL}}T_f$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{UL}}T_f$ respectively, with $\tau_{\mathrm{DL}}, \tau_{\mathrm{UL}} \in [0,1]$. We consider $N_{\mathrm{DL}}$ available \gls{ofdm} symbols in \gls{dl} and $N_{\mathrm{UL}}$ in \gls{ul}, of which $N_{\mathrm{a, DL}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{a, UL}}$ are activated and carrying data. The activated fraction is the average physical resource load
\begin{align}
\label{eq:load}
\bar{x}_{i} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{a},i}}{N_{i}} \in [0,1] \quad i \in \{\mathrm{DL}, \mathrm{UL}\},
\end{align}
so $\bar{x}_{i}=1$ is a fully loaded direction and $\bar{x}_{i}=0$ one that carries reference signals only. In general, as defined in~\cite{peschiera2026fr3}, the load also counts how many \glspl{ue} are served and how many subcarriers each of them is allocated. We assume throughout that every activated symbol serves all $K$ \glspl{ue} on all $Q$ subcarriers, so the load reduces to the ratio of activated to available symbols. The \gls{ul} and \gls{dl} frames are further subdevided into three parts as depicted in \todo{add fig and ref it}. The first part is for reference signaling and has length
\begin{align}
    \tau_i \tau_{\mathrm{sig}, i} \quad i \in \{\mathrm{DL}, \mathrm{UL}\}, 
\end{align}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{sig}, i} \in [0,1]$ is the fraction of the frame used for signaling. The second part is for data transmission and has length
\begin{align}
\label{eq:frame_data}
    \tau_i (1 - \tau_{\mathrm{sig}, i}) \bar{x}_{i}\quad i \in \{\mathrm{DL}, \mathrm{UL}\}.
\end{align}
The third part represents a micro-sleep or \gls{dtx} mode~\cite{3GPP_TR38864} and has length
\begin{align}
   \tau_i (1 - \tau_{\mathrm{sig}, i})(1 - \bar{x}_{i})\quad i \in \{\mathrm{DL}, \mathrm{UL}\}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Signal Model}
\label{ssec:signal}

Let $\vect{h}_{kl}[q]\inC{M}$ be the channel from \gls{ap} $l$ to \gls{ue} $k$.
Stacking the \glspl{ap} gives the collective channel
$\vect{h}_{k}[q]=[\,\vect{h}_{k1}[q]^{\intercal}\ \cdots\
\vect{h}_{kL}[q]^{\intercal}\,]^{\intercal}\inC{LM}$ and
$\mat{H}[q]=[\,\vect{h}_{1}[q]\ \cdots\ \vect{h}_{K}[q]\,]\inC{LM\times K}$.


\subsubsection{Downlink}
\gls{ap} $l$ transmits $\vect{x}_{l}[q]=\sum_{k}\vect{w}_{kl}[q]s_{k}[q]$, with
$\vect{w}_{kl}[q]\inC{M}$ its precoding vector for \gls{ue} $k$ and $s_{k}[q]$
the unit-power data symbols, mutually independent across \glspl{ue}. Let $\vect{w}_{k}[q]\inC{LM}$ be the collection of the $L$ precoders of the \glspl{ap} $\vect{w}_{kl}[q]$, the
signal received by \gls{ue} $k$ is
\begin{align}
y_{k}[q]=\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{k}[q]s_{k}[q]
+\sum_{i\neq k}\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{i}[q]s_{i}[q]+n_{k}[q]
\end{align} 
with $n_{k}[q]\sim\cn{0}{\sigma^{2}/Q}$. Treating the multi-user interference as
noise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl-sinr}
\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{DL}}[q] =
\frac{\abs{\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{k}[q]}^{2}}
{\sum_{i\neq k}\abs{\vect{h}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{w}_{i}[q]}^{2} + \sigma^{2}/Q} ,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{2}$ is the receiver noise power. 
\subsubsection{Uplink}
\gls{ue} $k$ transmits with power $p_{k}[q]=p_{k}/Q$ subject to its own budget
$p_{k}\leq p_{\max}$, and \gls{ap} $l$ observes
\begin{align}
\vect{y}_{l}[q]=\sum_{k}\sqrt{p_{k}[q]}\,\vect{h}_{kl}[q]s_{k}[q]+\vect{n}_{l}[q]\end{align}
with $\vect{n}_{l}[q]\sim\cn{\vect{0}}{(\sigma^{2}/Q)\mat{I}_{M}}$. Applying a receive combiner $\vect{v}_{k}[q]\inC{LM}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ul-distributive}
\hat{s}_{k}[q] = \vect{v}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{y}[q]
= \sum_{l=1}^{L}\vect{v}_{kl}[q]^{H}\vect{y}_{l}[q] .
\end{equation}
%Linear combining can therefor be performed per \glspl{ap}, i.e., in distributed operation an \gls{ap} holding its own combining coefficients forms its partial sum locally and forwards one scalar per \gls{ue} instead of $M$ sample streams. 
The resulting \gls{sinr} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ul-sinr}
\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{UL}}[q] =
\frac{p_{k}\abs{\vect{v}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{h}_{k}[q]}^{2}}
{\sum_{i\neq k}p_{i}\abs{\vect{v}_{k}[q]^{H}\vect{h}_{i}[q]}^{2}
+ \sigma^{2}\norm{\vect{v}_{k}[q]}^{2}}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Channel and Noise Model}
\label{ssec:channel}

Channels are drawn from the 3GPP TR 38.901 urban microcell model \cite{tr38901}
through the Sionna implementation \cite{hoydis2022sionna}. Each \gls{ap} is a
38.901 base station carrying an $M$-element half-wavelength \gls{ula} of
omnidirectional elements at height $h_{\mathrm{AP}}$, and each \gls{ue} is a
single-antenna outdoor terminal at height $h_{\mathrm{UE}}$. \glspl{ue} are
dropped uniformly over a square coverage area of side $D$, as are the \glspl{ap}.
In the co-located case a single \gls{bs} is placed at the centre. The receiver noise power follows from the thermal noise density,
$\sigma^{2}\,[\mathrm{dBm}] = -174 + 10\log_{10}(B/\mathrm{Hz}) + F$, with $B$
the bandwidth and $F$ the noise figure in dB.

The model is specified through realizations rather than a closed-form correlation
matrix, so the large-scale fading coefficient $\beta_{kl}$, used for power control, is estimated from the realization itself as 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:beta}
\beta_{kl} = \frac{1}{MQ}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\norm{\vect{h}_{kl}[q]}^{2}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Centralized and Distributed Operation}
\label{ssec:operation}

Unlike the co-located case, which has a single sum-power budget, each \gls{ap} has its own power constraint. Since the symbols are independent
and unit-power, the power radiated by \gls{ap} $l$ over the \gls{ofdm} block is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ptl}
P_{T,l} = \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\norm{\vect{w}_{kl}[q]}^{2} \leq \rho_{\max}.
\end{equation}
How \eqref{eq:ptl} is met is the difference between centralized and distributed operation, which is also related to the functional splits of \Cref{sssec:split}.

\subsubsection{Centralized operation (splits S1, S2)}
The \gls{cpu} holds $\mat{H}[q]$ and designs all precoders jointly. In the downlink it applies
\gls{zf},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zf-precoder}
\bar{\mat{W}}[q] = \mat{H}[q]\big(\mat{H}[q]^{H}\mat{H}[q]\big)^{-1}
\inC{LM\times K},
\end{equation}
which nulls all inter-user interference and requires $K\leq LM$. A single power
coefficient $\rho_{k}$ is attached to each \gls{ue}, since the precoding direction already fixes how that power spreads over the $LM$ antennas. Fractional power control weights the \glspl{ue} by their aggregate gain $\beta_{k}=\sum_{l}\beta_{kl}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dl-fractional}
\rho_{k} = P_{\mathrm{tot}}\,\frac{\beta_{k}^{\,v}}{\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{\,v}},
\qquad P_{\mathrm{tot}} = L\rho_{\max},
\end{equation}
with the exponent $v$ trading throughput against fairness\footnote{A positive $v$ follows the channel and serves the \glspl{ue} with the largest aggregate gain first, which maximizes the sum rate but starves those at the edge of the coverage area. A negative $v$ compensates the weak \glspl{ue}: at $v=-1$ the product
$\rho_{k}\beta_{k}$ is the same for every \gls{ue}, so all of them are received
at approximately equal signal power, which is the fair extreme. Values in
$[-1,1]$ therefore interpolate between a throughput-optimal and a max-min-fair
allocation.}. 
This power normalization is applied as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:power-normalization}
\vect{w}_{k}[q] = \gamma\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\,
\frac{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q]}{\sqrt{\sum_{q'}\norm{\bar{\vect{w}}_{k}[q']}^{2}}},
\qquad
\gamma = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\max}}{\max_{l}\tilde{p}_{l}}},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde p_{l}$ is the power \gls{ap} $l$ would radiate before that
rescaling. Only the busiest \gls{ap} then reaches its budget. Since
$\sum_{k}\rho_{k}=L\rho_{\max}$ the nominal per-\gls{ap} powers average to
$\rho_{\max}$, so $\gamma\leq 1$, the constraint is always met. The uplink counterpart forwards the raw
observations to the \gls{cpu}, which combines over all $LM$ antennas with
$\mat{V}[q]=\mat{H}[q](\mat{H}[q]^{H}\mat{H}[q])^{-1}$. 

\subsubsection{Distributed operation (split S3)}
Each \gls{ap} designs its own precoder/combiner from its local \gls{csi}, so it suppresses only the interference it generates or observes itself and the coherent cross-\gls{ap} cancellation is lost. Writing $\mat{E}_{l}[q]=[\,\vect{h}_{1l}[q]\ \cdots\
\vect{h}_{Kl}[q]\,]\inC{M\times K}$ for the local channel matrix and
$\mat{P}=\diag{p_{1},\dots,p_{K}}$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:local-rzf}
\mat{W}_{l}[q] &= \big(\mat{E}_{l}[q]\mat{E}_{l}[q]^{H}
+ \lambda\mat{I}_{M}\big)^{-1}\mat{E}_{l}[q], \\
\label{eq:ul-local-mmse}
\mat{V}_{l}[q] &= \big(\mat{E}_{l}[q]\mat{P}\mat{E}_{l}[q]^{H}
+ \lambda\mat{I}_{M}\big)^{-1}\mat{E}_{l}[q],
\end{align}
are the local \gls{rzf} precoder/combiner which correspond to and the local \gls{mmse} precoder/combiner, with $\lambda=\sigma^{2}$. Each \gls{ap} also allocates its own power budget among the \glspl{ue} from local statistics,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-fractional}
\rho_{kl} = \rho_{\max}\,\frac{\beta_{kl}^{\,v}}{\sum_{i}\beta_{il}^{\,v}},
\end{equation}
so $\sum_{k}\rho_{kl}=\rho_{\max}$ by construction and every \gls{ap} meets
\eqref{eq:ptl} with equality, $P_{T,l}=\rho_{\max}$, without any
\gls{cpu}-side rescaling. In
the uplink the \gls{cpu} fuses the $L$ local estimates by summing them,
$\hat s_{k}[q]=\sum_{l}\vect{v}_{kl}[q]^{H}\vect{y}_{l}[q]$.

In the uplink fractional power control is the same for both centralized and distributed operation at $p_{k}=p_{\max}\beta_{k}^{\,v_{\mathrm{ul}}}/\max_{i}\beta_{i}^{\,v_{\mathrm{ul}}}$. This is normalized by a maximum rather than a sum because each \gls{ue} has its own independent power budget.

\subsection{Delivered Rates}
\label{ssec:rates}

The per-\gls{ue} spectral efficiencies average over the subcarriers and over the
Monte Carlo channel realizations,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:se}
\mathrm{SE}_{k}^{i} = \tau_{i}(1-\tau_{\mathrm{sig},i})\,\bar{x}_{i}\,
\expt{\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}
\log_{2}\!\left(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{i}[q]\right)}
\end{align}
with $i\in\{\mathrm{DL},\mathrm{UL}\}$, the prelog
is the data fraction \eqref{eq:frame_data} of the frame, since reference
signalling and the deactivated symbols carry no payload. 
The delivered sum rates can then be written as $R_{i}= \tilde B \sum_{k}\mathrm{SE}_{k}^{i}$, with
the effective bandwidth $\tilde B = 0.9\,B$.
